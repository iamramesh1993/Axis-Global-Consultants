If you are choosing between a September 2026 and a January 2027 start in the UK,
this is the single fact that should shape the decision — and almost nobody in the
Pakistani market is putting it in front of students clearly.

## What is changing

The Graduate visa lets you stay in the UK and work without an employer sponsoring
you after you finish your course. Right now it runs for two years for bachelor's and
master's graduates.

From **1 January 2027**, that becomes **18 months**.

- Apply for the Graduate visa **on or before 31 December 2026** → **2 years**
- Apply **on or after 1 January 2027** → **18 months**
- Completed a PhD or other doctoral qualification → **3 years**, either way

## The part people get wrong

The rule turns on **the date you apply for the Graduate visa**. Not the date you
started your degree. Not the date you got your offer. Not the date you arrived.

You cannot apply until you have finished your course and your university has
confirmed your completion to the Home Office. And you must be inside the UK when
you apply.

So work backwards from your course end date:

- A **September 2025** master's start typically finishes around September 2026. You can apply in autumn 2026 and get **two years**.
- A **January 2026** master's start typically finishes in early 2027. You will be applying after the cutoff, so **18 months**.
- A **September 2026** master's start finishes around September 2027. **18 months**.

Read that middle line again. A January 2026 start and a September 2026 start land in
the same place, but a September 2025 start does not. The gap between two years and
eighteen months can come down to a single semester's timing.

## Does six months actually matter?

It depends entirely on what you are trying to do.

**It matters a great deal if** your plan is to convert post-study work into a
Skilled Worker visa. Graduate schemes at large UK employers run on annual cycles.
Two years means you can realistically sit two recruitment rounds. Eighteen months
can mean one and a half — and if you finish in autumn, the second round may close
just after your visa expires. That six months is often the difference between one
shot and two.

**It matters less if** you are returning to Pakistan regardless, or you already have
a UK employer lined up, or you are heading to a doctoral programme where nothing
changes.

## What has not changed

Worth stating, because the noise around UK immigration makes people assume the worst:

- The Student visa itself is unchanged in duration
- You can still work 20 hours a week in term time on a degree-level course
- Doctoral graduates still get three years
- The Graduate Route still requires no employer sponsorship and no minimum salary

The route is getting shorter. It is not going away.
