Almost every refusal we are asked to review after the fact comes down to money.
Rarely a shortage of it. Usually the documentation of it.

That distinction matters, because a family that genuinely has the funds and gets
refused anyway has lost the visa fee, a semester, and sometimes the deposit — for a
paperwork problem that was entirely avoidable.

Here are the five we see most.

## 1. Breaking the 28-day rule without realising

For the UK, the required amount must sit in the account at or above the threshold
for **28 consecutive days**, and the closing balance on your statement must be dated
within **31 days** of the day you apply.

Where this goes wrong:

- The balance dips below the threshold on one single day — a utility payment, a card transaction — and the 28-day clock restarts from zero
- The statement is a few days too old by the time the application is actually submitted
- Currency movement pushes the equivalent below the threshold on a day nobody was watching

Fix it by holding a **buffer above the minimum**, not the exact figure, and by not
touching the account at all during those 28 days. If you are converting from rupees,
leave headroom for the rate to move against you.

## 2. Showing the living-cost figure and nothing else

Every one of these countries asks for living costs **plus tuition plus travel**. The
headline number is only the first component.

- **UK:** unpaid tuition on your CAS, plus £1,529 a month in London or £1,171 outside, for up to nine months
- **Canada:** CAD 22,895 in living costs for a single applicant, **plus** first-year tuition, **plus** return airfare
- **Australia:** AUD 29,710 in living costs for twelve months, **plus** tuition, **plus** travel

Families read "CAD 22,895" and prepare exactly that. For a CAD 20,000 programme the
actual requirement is closer to CAD 43,000 plus flights. This is the single most
common under-preparation we see on Canada files.

## 3. Money held by the wrong person

For the UK this is strict: funds should be in your own name, or your parents' or
legal guardian's, with their written consent and documentary proof of the
relationship. An uncle, an elder brother, a family friend, or a family business
account are generally **not** acceptable, however genuine the support.

Canada and Australia are somewhat more flexible, but "flexible" means they will
assess the relationship and the source — not that anything goes.

If the money genuinely sits with a relative outside the accepted categories, move it
into an accepted account **and then start the holding period**. Do not apply and hope
the relationship is overlooked.

## 4. A large deposit with no story

A single large credit shortly before you apply is not fatal, but it shifts the
question from _do you have the money_ to _where did this come from_.

Legitimate sources are common in Pakistan — a plot sale, a gratuity or pension
payout, an inheritance, the maturity of a committee. All are fine. What is not fine
is having no paperwork for it.

Bring the sale deed, the employer letter, the succession document. And move the money
**before** the 28-day window opens, so the deposit sits outside the period being
examined rather than inside it.

## 5. Treating part-time work as part of the plan

You can work 20 hours a week in the UK on a degree course, 24 hours a week in
Canada, and 48 hours a fortnight in Australia. None of that counts towards proving
you can afford to study.

The financial tests are deliberately built on the assumption that you are **not**
relying on term-time earnings. Any application whose budget only balances once
part-time income is included is not a funded application. Assessors have seen the
spreadsheet before.

## What good looks like

A file we are comfortable submitting usually has:

- Funds in an accepted holder's name, **three to four months** before application, not four weeks
- A balance **10–15% above** the minimum, to absorb exchange-rate movement
- Documented provenance for anything unusual
- Tuition and travel budgeted **separately** from the living-cost threshold
- Bank statements that are original, stamped, and continuous with no gaps
