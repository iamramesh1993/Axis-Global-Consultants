## Why students pick Canada

Two reasons, usually: the post-graduation work permit, and the sense that Canada
is more open to people staying. Both still have substance. Neither is as automatic
as it was five years ago, and pretending otherwise is how students end up with a
diploma that leads nowhere.

Canada is also genuinely good value at the undergraduate level compared with the
US or Australia, and public colleges offer applied programmes with real employment
outcomes — if you pick a programme that still qualifies for a work permit.

## What it actually costs

Undergraduate tuition for international students generally runs CAD 17,000 to
CAD 40,000 a year, with public colleges at the lower end and professional or
engineering degrees at the top.

On the visa side, the number that matters is CAD 22,895. That is the living-cost
threshold for a single applicant outside Quebec, and it sits **on top of** your
first year of tuition and your travel costs. So a CAD 20,000 programme means
demonstrating roughly CAD 43,000 in total, plus airfare.

This is the number families underestimate. Budget from it, not from a college's
own cost-of-living estimate.

## The cap, and what a PAL really is

Canada limits how many study permits it will approve, and distributes attestation
letters to institutions within provincial allocations. Your institution requests
the PAL on your behalf once you have accepted an offer and, usually, paid a
deposit.

Two things follow. First, an acceptance letter is not a PAL, and a PAL is not a
study permit — they are three separate documents in sequence. Second, no
consultant anywhere can guarantee you a PAL, because no consultant controls the
allocation. If you are told otherwise, stop.

## Where SDS went

The Student Direct Stream closed in November 2024. There is now one study permit
stream for everyone. A GIC remains a clean and well-understood way to show funds,
and many families still use one for exactly that reason — but it is now a choice,
not the price of admission to a fast lane.

We mention this because it is a live test of whether your advisor is current. The
SDS has been gone for well over a year.

## Work rights, during and after

During term you can work up to 24 hours a week off campus, and unlimited hours
during scheduled breaks. That helps with living costs. It does not fund tuition,
and the financial rules assume it does not.

The post-graduation work permit runs up to three years depending on your
programme's length. Eligibility now depends on which programme and which
institution — several college programmes have been carved out, and language
requirements apply to some applicants. Confirm the eligibility of the specific
programme before you commit money to it.
