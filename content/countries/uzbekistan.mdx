## Read this part first

Most pages about MBBS in Uzbekistan open with how affordable it is. We are going to
open somewhere else, because the price is not the thing that decides whether this
works out for you.

**A medical degree is only worth what you can do with it.** For a Pakistani student
who intends to come home and practise, that means three separate gates, all of
which must be cleared:

1. A **PMDC No Objection Certificate**, obtained _before_ you leave Pakistan.
2. A university with **appropriate accreditation** — WFME recognition is the
   benchmark to check, not a WHO directory listing.
3. Passing Pakistan's **equivalence and licensing examinations** when you return.

Miss the first and you may hold a degree you cannot register. Miss the third and you
hold a degree you cannot use. Neither has a workaround, and neither is fixed by
paying anyone a fee.

A large proportion of foreign-qualified graduates do not pass Pakistan's licensing
examinations at the first attempt. That is the single most important number on this
page, and it is the one you will not find in a brochure.

## What Uzbekistan genuinely offers

With that said, the appeal is real and we are not here to talk you out of something
that fits.

Tuition of USD 3,500 to USD 6,000 a year and living costs around USD 200 a month
put a six-year medical degree within reach of families for whom the UK, Canada or
Australia are simply not options. Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara, Andijan and
Fergana host long-established medical institutes. Flights are short, the cultural
distance is small, and there is an established Pakistani student community.

For a student who missed a Pakistani seat by a narrow margin, has a valid MDCAT
result, and is prepared to work towards the licensing exam from first year, this can
be a sound decision.

## What it costs, fully loaded

- **Tuition:** USD 3,500 – 6,000 per year, so roughly USD 21,000 – 36,000 over six years
- **Hostel and food:** about USD 150 – 250 per month
- **Travel:** two to four flights a year, plus visa costs
- **On return:** equivalence and licensing exam fees, registration, and realistically a period of dedicated preparation

Budget USD 32,000 – 54,000 for the degree itself, and treat the exam preparation
after it as part of the plan rather than an afterthought.

## Structure of the degree

Six years: typically five years of academic and pre-clinical study followed by a
one-year internship. English-medium streams exist at several universities, but you
should get that confirmed in writing for your specific cohort, and expect to pick up
functional Uzbek or Russian for clinical rotations regardless of the language of
lectures.
